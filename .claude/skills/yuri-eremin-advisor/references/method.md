# Редакционный метод

This reference is a source-grounded synthesis of the available recordings, transcripts, course materials, and verified public content. It is not a verbatim or official methodology published by Yuri Eremin.

## Core objective

For a premium architectural practice, content should help a potential client answer three questions quickly:

1. Who is this person?
2. Can I trust this person with the complexity of my house and way of life?
3. What should I do next if the answer is yes?

The portfolio proves capability, but the recognizable author, his decisions, and the client outcome create trust.

## Ten working rules

1. Give each item one function: discovery, trust, proof, or conversion.
2. Begin with a concrete tension, risk, contrast, or meaningful promise; avoid a general preamble.
3. Establish the human stakes immediately: comfort, privacy, daily routine, confidence, time, money, durability, or emotional experience.
4. Speak from the author's practice: `я создаю`, `я учитываю`, `в моих проектах`. For DEEV Reels, never use second-person address, including the CTA; make the relevant person legible through a concrete client situation.
5. Explain professional decisions through their effect on the client's life, not as a lesson for self-design.
6. Use biography only when it proves character, continuity, judgment, or the reason behind the work.
7. Put the finished client outcome above credentials, document volume, and process terminology.
8. Make the visuals prove the spoken idea even with the sound off; use the author, real work, drawings, objects, and details relevant to the sentence.
9. Keep one narrative line: hook → meaning or tension → author's response → client result → one next action.
10. Finish only after value has been established. The CTA must continue the topic instead of behaving like an advertising attachment.

Use one strict content unit whenever possible: one lived scenario → one problem → one authorial decision → one client result. A broad room tour or product overview usually needs to be split into separate ideas.

Match subject depth to relationship stage. A cold viewer needs a broadly recognizable situation and a reason to remember the author; a warmer viewer can use project evidence, working detail, and a direct route to contact.

When explaining a professional tool, connect it to the decision it helps verify, the expense or error it helps prevent, and the confidence it gives the client. Never let the tool become the product.

State the positive result before the burden removed. `Цельный реализованный дом` is the value; `клиенту не приходится сводить специалистов` is supporting proof of responsibility.

## Author-and-addressee check

For material about Vyacheslav's professional method, test the complete causal chain:

`why I do this → how I do it → what experience produced the method → what professional confidence it gives me → what result the client receives → how the client's life changes`

The chain is a completeness test, not a mandatory sentence template. The opening may remain compact, but the finished material must make the author, addressee, reason, and lived client outcome clear. Rewrite every second-person construction from the author's position or name the recognizable client situation.

Test the emotional distance as well as grammatical clarity. Preserve the hook's tension while making the delivery sound like a natural observation from Vyacheslav, never as a direct command or personal claim on the viewer.

## Blog-versus-advertisement check

Do not let a project turn the Reel into an advertisement for the object. The blog's material must reveal an authorial observation, decision, experience, or point of view. Use the project as proof of that thought. If the finished edit could be replaced by a generic studio promo without losing its meaning, restore the author and the actual professional reasoning.

## Client-first communication

Do not lead with awards, team structure, production facilities, packages, price per square metre, or portfolio volume before the client's desired life and result are legible. These facts may prove capability later; they are not a substitute for understanding the person.

Translate internal service terms into a clear answer to `what will be created for this client and why it matters`. A portfolio demonstrates judgment and range; do not make the client choose a ready-made life from someone else's interior. Treat clarification questions as evidence that the offer is still unclear, not as a reason to label the person difficult.

When a contrast story fits the topic, a useful narrative mechanism is:

`desired future → real cost or work of the path → avoidance → consequence`

Use it without ridicule or broad social judgment. For DEEV, the purpose is to make a choice and its consequence understandable, not to shame the viewer.

## Production discipline

Treat content as a protected production function. A course session, additional learning, client work, or discomfort does not itself produce an editorial result. Measure progress by material recorded, edited, published, and adapted to the relevant route. Reserve time and real production resources; do not generalize a one-off budget, filming duration, equipment list, or posting count into a universal requirement.

Do not count a perfunctory submission as meaningful practice. A usable iteration should apply the latest feedback in a visible way. When Yuri confirms that a direction works, continue producing and refining it instead of indefinitely rebuilding the same acceptable draft.

Separate desire from practice. Wanting reach, recognition, or professional growth does not count as movement until the necessary work is accepted and performed.

Treat marketing as a test cycle rather than a guarantee from one publication: `hypothesis → publish → inspect response and lead quality → learn → next iteration`. Stop or change a test that attracts the wrong audience or weak leads; extend a format that produces the intended response. A single private example of posting volume or time-to-result is evidence of persistence, not a universal quota or promise.

## Interview-to-series format

The 16 September group assignment adds a repeatable production format:

`one ordinary person's question → one natural author answer → one Reel`

Use real questions about materials, choices, trends, daily situations, and visible consequences. Do not frame the question as a defence of the profession—`why a designer, project, or procurement service is necessary`. Record without pre-agreeing the answer, keep Vyacheslav's natural language, remove only dead pauses and irrelevant material, and add subtitles plus visuals only when they clarify the answer. One conversation may supply a series and a reserve of future items. The meeting's specific five-published-plus-five-in-reserve assignment was a one-week exercise, not a standing quota.

## Eight prohibitions

- Do not turn the material into a checklist that teaches the viewer to replace the architect.
- Do not address the viewer in the second person or speak down to them.
- Do not start with a long biography, geography, awards, or a generic definition.
- Do not describe a process without saying what changes for the client.
- Do not turn the client into a co-designer or coordinator through vague promises to `help create` the result.
- Do not put professional speech over unrelated lifestyle footage.
- Do not use unverified numbers, achievements, guarantees, or claims of exclusivity.
- Do not add `подпишитесь`, `поставьте лайк`, or `напишите мне` without a topic-specific reason.
- Do not copy Yuri's sales vocabulary, provocative comparisons, or mannerisms into DEEV's voice.

## Hook check

A strong opening passes all four checks:

- It is understandable in the first short phrase.
- It creates a relevant question for a potential client, not only for colleagues.
- The rest of the video answers exactly that question.
- The opening can be supported by the first visual frame.

Before using a problem hook, confirm that the intended client would recognize the consequence as undesirable. A professional's dislike, a trend label, or a colleague's technical concern is not automatically a client problem. Begin with the author's own observation or a specific scene, without direct address.

When a concrete problem is already known, name it in the first meaningful phrase instead of explaining the planned series or approaching it through a long conceptual preamble. Then connect the problem to its cause, Vyacheslav's response, and the client's outcome. Directness must remain natural and non-accusatory.

For a pinned `Кто я` Reel, a direct name-and-role opening may be retained when profile orientation is more important than cold reach. In that case the second sentence must immediately add proof, tension, or a distinctive point of view.

## CTA construction

Build the ending from four parts:

1. **Available route:** the exact button, form, message, pinned item, or page that really exists.
2. **Single action:** one verb and one task only.
3. **Easy content:** tell the client what small piece of information to provide; do not ask for a full brief.
4. **Relevant return:** state what the bureau will clarify, reveal, or take responsibility for next. Never promise a service that does not exist.

For DEEV Reels, phrase the CTA without second-person pronouns or imperatives. Prefer a neutral route such as `обсудить новый проект можно по кнопке связи в профиле`.

For a premium `Кто я` Reel, prefer a small human input—what matters most in the future home, how the person wants to live, or what the home should make possible—over administrative qualification such as project type, square metres, budget, or stage. The response must express value for the client's life or confidence, not merely promise a scope, estimate, meeting, or internal process.

The client supplies the priority, desired life, and honest reaction—not the architectural answer. Make the author's interpretive responsibility explicit: turn that input into spatial decisions and preserve their logic through the agreed scope.

Good CTA logic:

`реальная кнопка → один содержательный вопрос → понятный профессиональный ответ`

Weak CTA logic:

`если готовы → напишите → обсудим → предложу следующий шаг`

The weak version postpones meaning, asks for readiness already demonstrated by watching, and gives no reason to act now.

Match the CTA to the viewer's stage. A discovery Reel may use a specific reason to follow and encounter the author again. A pinned self-presentation may lead to contact because the profile context has already supplied identity and trust. Do not force a contact CTA onto every broad-reach Reel.

### CTA approval questions

Approve only if every answer is yes:

1. Has the viewer already received value or proof?
2. Is there exactly one action?
3. Does the action correspond to a real interface or communication route?
4. Is it obvious what the person should say, send, view, or choose?
5. Is the promised response real and within the bureau's scope?
6. Does the ending extend this video's central idea?
7. Is it free of pressure, fake urgency, and generic engagement bait?
8. Does it avoid repeating the preceding conclusion?
9. Can it be spoken naturally in one breath?
10. Does it ask about the client's desired life or result rather than make the client prepare the architect's brief?

## Spoken-language check

- Prefer one thought per sentence.
- Use active verbs and concrete nouns.
- Replace `осуществлять`, `являться`, `в рамках`, and stacked abstractions.
- Reject ornamental luxury abstractions that a client would not use in ordinary speech.
- Remove connective filler: `ну`, `вот`, `значит`, `как бы`.
- Avoid adjacent repetitions of `дом`, `интерьер`, `архитектура`, and `проект` when a pronoun or a more precise object works.
- Preserve Vyacheslav's directness; do not polish away the personal phrase `меня буквально затолкнули в архитектуру` when he has approved it.
- Read numbers and dates in the way a person naturally says them aloud.

## Visual baseline

For most DEEV Reels, use voice-over or direct speech, the author's face, relevant archival or current project materials, and accurate subtitles. A practical sequence is: author → biographical proof → professional work → finished spatial evidence → direct closing frame. Do not use stock work as if it were DEEV's.

Run a silent-edit test before approval. The topic, author, and progression should remain legible without audio. Keep the author's face where it adds identity or emphasis—usually the hook, a meaningful turn, or the close—and let relevant evidence carry the remaining voice-over.

A repeatable visual and verbal frame may build recognition, but do not freeze it into a mechanical template. On a visit, event, or site, a small target such as three genuinely usable shots is enough; write and record the final thought afterward when that produces clearer work.

## Client-journey consistency

A strong Reel cannot compensate for a contradictory profile or an unclear first response. Check the complete route:

`first frame → Reel → profile → contact button or message → response → meeting`

At every transition, the same author, scope, client value, and level of care should remain legible. After viewing, the person should be able to give a one-sentence `dry residue`:

- who Vyacheslav is;
- what he creates;
- what the client receives;
- why this responsibility can be trusted to him;
- what action is available now.

Keep one primary professional role legible in each profile or piece. Supporting roles, tools, and methods must reinforce that role instead of competing with it.

Treat the pinned materials as a small connected system when several slots are available: `кто я → что я создаю → какой завершённый результат получает клиент`. Do not overload the first Reel with every service, method, and proof point.

Treat response clarity and reasonable acknowledgement speed as part of the brand. Do not leave a potential client in an information vacuum after a CTA succeeds.

## Metrics appropriate to the task

Judge a pinned self-presentation by qualified profile continuation, meaningful contact-button taps, project views, follows after viewing, and suitable client inquiries—not only reach or likes.
