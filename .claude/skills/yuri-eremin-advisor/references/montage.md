## Assembly order

Use this working order when it fits the material:

`finished script → voice-over → music → references → footage selection and arrangement → effects and filters → subtitles → sound design → final review`

Meaning precedes technique. Montage cannot repair an unclear author, reason, client result, or narrative line.

## References

- Save a specific device and the attention problem it solves, not only a whole admired Reel.
- Recreate useful shots and mechanisms on test footage; saving alone does not build the skill.
- Adapt the mechanism to DEEV's topic, footage, typography, pace, and voice.
- Never present third-party footage as DEEV work. Check attribution and usage rights before publishing.

## Footage bank

- Record professional daily life, projects, travel between work locations, details, face, hands, objects, and actions continuously.
- Capture one action from several distances and angles: wide, medium, close, and detail.
- Before editing, collect all potentially relevant footage in one album.
- Prefer a first frame with strong light, quality, composition, and action; use the author's face when recognition matters.
- Use archive footage only when it has a clear semantic and emotional relationship to the sentence.

## Frame arrangement

- Build the visual track against the voice-over's semantic phrases.
- Change the image when the thought changes.
- Alternate shot sizes, gaze direction, movement, and object detail; avoid a row of visually identical shots.
- Synchronize meaningful movements and appearances to the music when useful.
- A 0.5–1.5-second shot, sometimes up to 2 seconds, is a course-specific starting range for a dynamic Reel, not a platform law.
- Chronology may be incomplete, but unexplained jumps between incompatible seasons, places, or moods should be rejected.

For DEEV, map each section before assembly:

`spoken phrase → visual proof → shot size → action → sound or silence`

## Effects and filters

Use an effect only to direct attention, mark a turn, create atmosphere, or clarify a relationship.

Useful functions include:

- briefly removing music before a key line;
- freezing a meaningful frame;
- changing contrast or color for a deliberate emotional shift;
- placing a short key thought on a neutral background;
- connecting shots through a screen, frame, overlay, or matched movement;
- supporting a transition with one quiet repeated sound.

Prefer a small recognizable system of recurring devices over novelty. Remove any effect that cannot be justified by meaning, proof, or attention.

## Subtitles and typography

- Generate automatic subtitles as a temporary timing layer.
- Keep ordinary text in one stable safe area so the eye does not chase it.
- Typeset only important words or phrases manually, using size, weight, or one compatible secondary face to show hierarchy.
- Preserve alignment, consistent sizing logic, safe interface margins, and deliberate letter and line spacing.
- Match each word's onset to the voice; end the elements of one phrase together.
- Reserve complex type animation for rare emphasis. Do not turn the whole Reel into a typography demonstration.

## Voice-over

- Record in a quiet space.
- Speak in complete semantic paragraphs, not isolated words.
- Decide the paragraph's intonation, record several complete takes, and select the strongest.
- Use natural gesture and breath when they make the thought more alive.
- Do not rush or hold the microphone too close.
- Remove technical pauses while preserving a human rhythm.

## Sound design

- Review the picture without speech or music and identify only actions that naturally suggest sound.
- Add sounds for actions, transitions, or appearances when they improve atmosphere or legibility.
- Keep effects below the voice and music.
- Reuse a limited sound palette for coherence.
- Fade music deliberately and use silence as an accent.
