# Логика и язык исходных материалов

This is an analytical profile of recurring public and recorded patterns. It is not a personality model and must not be used to impersonate Yuri Eremin.

## Root diagnosis

The recurring marketing problem is not a designer's inability to produce drawings. A potential client usually cannot evaluate technical depth before engagement and does not choose a premium professional from beautiful images alone. The communication task is therefore to make three invisible qualities legible:

- the author notices the client's real situation, not only the visible object;
- the author does more than `listen`: he interprets the client's words and translates them into decisions;
- the author can carry responsibility through complexity;
- the client receives a coherent lived result and greater confidence, not merely documents.

For a premium client, the recurring underlying anxiety is wasted time and money, an outcome that does not match the intended life, and the prospect of personally reconciling many disconnected specialists. Content should make the author's responsibility visible without manufacturing fear or dependence.

This explains why the source material repeatedly moves from portfolio to recognizable author, from process to outcome, and from general expertise to one concrete client consequence.

## Recurring reasoning moves

1. State a familiar belief or surface explanation.
2. Break it with a direct contradiction or an uncomfortable question.
3. Replace abstraction with one visible example or everyday consequence.
4. Show the author's own solution or stance.
5. Translate it into the client's result.
6. Ask for one action whose meaning has already been earned by the content.

Biography and famous stories are used as evidence for a point, not as an independent chronology. A statistic or ranking is useful only when it changes the viewer's understanding or decision.

## Surface language in the sources

The recorded language is conversational and often improvised. It uses:

- short rhetorical questions;
- blunt contrasts such as `кажется / на самом деле`;
- repetition to stress the main thought;
- concrete losses, inconveniences, and outcomes;
- direct second-person address;
- a closing action tied to a declared purpose.

Transcripts also contain filler, unfinished sentences, colloquialisms, and deliberately provocative sales language. These are properties of live speech, not mandatory editorial techniques.

## Translation into DEEV voice

Preserve:

- the sharp diagnosis;
- the clear contrast;
- the visible consequence;
- the author's responsibility;
- the client outcome;
- one meaningful action.

Transform:

- provocation into calm certainty;
- mass-market comparison into precise architectural language;
- long improvisation into short spoken sentences;
- generic lead-generation language into a real next step;
- `you are doing it wrong` into `here is what I take into account for my clients`.
- source-style direct second-person address into first-person authorship, a named client situation, or a neutral route to contact; DEEV Reels do not use second person even in the CTA.

Prefer verbs that preserve professional responsibility: `создаю`, `проектирую`, `связываю`, `довожу`, `отвечаю`. Use `помогаю создать` or `мы вместе создаём` only when shared authorship is factually intended; otherwise those phrases quietly return professional work to the client. The client defines priorities and approves decisions but should not be framed as the bureau's coordinator, designer, or site controller.

Test abstract promises against a concrete future day: how the person arrives, rests, receives guests, finds privacy, uses the view, or moves through the house. Use the scene in content only when it is true for the project; its purpose is to expose vague language, not invent a lifestyle for the client.

The resulting voice should sound like an experienced architect speaking plainly: assured but not loud, personal but not confessional, exact but not academic.

## Test for the root problem

Before approving content, ask:

1. What client uncertainty does this remove?
2. What responsibility becomes visible?
3. What result can the client picture in daily life?
4. Why does this material make Vyacheslav—not an anonymous portfolio—the relevant person?

If the answers are only `experience`, `taste`, `drawings`, `beautiful design`, or abstract luxury language, the message has not reached the root problem.
